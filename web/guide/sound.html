What makes a synth track sound the way it does, and where each control is. The builder reference, BUILDER.md, lists what each engine costs and how to leave it out. Names in capitals are the labels on the screen.

Engines

A new project (power-on, PROJECT > NEW) starts with T1 on ANALOG ACID 303, T2 on FM6 GLASS EP (the KEYS voice pack) and T3 on DIGITAL GLASS PAD. A build without the engine or the pack plays a fallback instead: T2 DIGITAL RHODES without FM6 or its KEYS pack, T1 the first engine built, T3 the lead of earlier versions (SAMPLE LOFI FLUTE, or ANALOG G-FUNK LD where the FLUTE set is left out).

A synth track plays one engine at a time. PRESETS on a synth track loads another sound of the track's engine; SHIFT (OCT- held) + PRESETS (or the ENGINE card) steps the engine. Every engine is a builder item (ENG_*). A build without an engine plays a track that uses it on a stand-in engine and shows MISSING: DIGITAL stands in for FM6, PHASE for CZ and VPS, SAMPLE for GRAIN and SLICE, LOFI for SID, ANALOG for the others (BUILDER.md names each stand-in).

EDIT (tapped) opens the track's sound. Its first row, SOUND, has the ENGINE, PRESET, INIT and SAVE AS cards. Then MAIN: the four values that matter most for the engine (CUT and RES first where it has a filter; FM6 has LPF, RES and HPF). MAIN is there only when the engine has three pages of its own or more (ANALOG, for example). With one or two, EDIT opens on the first of them. Then the engine's own values on EDIT 1 and EDIT 2, four cards each, with the page titles the engine gives them (ANALOG shows OSC and FLT, for example).

Engine notes

  • ANALOG 2: OSC 2 has WAVE2, SEMI (osc 2's interval, +-24 semitones) and SYNC (hard sync; the SHP modulation then sweeps osc 2); SWARM has SWARM (0 to 6 detuned copies, the supersaw), SDTN (their spread) and DRFT (a slow random drift); FLT 2 has FTYP (LP12, LP24, BP, HP). The filter saturates in its loop and self-oscillates at RES 126-127. With any of the builder items A2_LADR, A2_OTA, A2_DIOD, A2_MS20 built, FTYP also has NOTCH and FLT 2 has MODEL: SVF (the filter above), LADR (a Moog-style transistor ladder: drive grits it, the bass thins as RES rises), OTA (a Juno-style 4-pole: clean input, a round resonance), DIOD (a 303-style diode ladder: squelchy), MS20 (a Sallen-Key 2-pole that screams). Every model takes every FTYP and rings in tune at the top of RES. MODEL and FTYP are one stored value, so a lock or a motion event on FTYP carries the model too; a sound asking for a model the build leaves out plays the SVF in the same mode. FLT B is a second filter before FLT (the MS-20's order: a high-pass, then the low-pass): MODEL (OFF, SVF, then the models built), TYPE (LP12 LP24 BP HP NOTCH), FREQ, RES; OFF HP by default, and OFF costs nothing. Its FREQ has no key tracking and no ENV 1 depth: move it with DEST FLT B.
  • FM6: DX7 voices played as Dexed plays them. VOICE: INIT VOICE and the voice packs the build carries (FM6_PK_*, 16 voices each, programmed for Optimist: K keys, B basses, M bells, P pads and leads, 8 80s classics), listed in PRESETS after the user presets. FILT is a filter on the part after FM6: LPF closes a low-pass from 20 kHz, RES gives it a peak, HPF opens a high-pass; 0 is off for LPF and HPF, and ENV 1 can sweep the LPF. ENV held on an FM6 track is the operator editor: the black keys pick OP1 to OP6, PIT, GLO, MONO, POLY, the knobs edit, the white keys keep playing. ENV tapped shows the same pages as rows (OPERATOR, then the six pages of the operator picked: TUNE, RATES, LEVELS, SENS, SCALE, CURVES; PIT; ALGO, LFO, LFO 2, PORTA). On those rows SHIFT2 + a black key picks the operator and SHIFT + an operator's key mutes it. An edited voice is kept as a user preset (SAVE AS) or by the project. DX7 SysEx in (FM6_SYSEX, receive only): a single voice goes into the selected FM6 track; a 32-voice bank becomes a temporary pick list at the end of PRESETS until power-off.
  • ACID: one TB-303-style voice; a step's ACCENT is the 303's accent, a SLIDE glides into the next step. ACID GEN (EDIT): DENS, ACC, SLD, then GEN twice writes a new acid line into the pattern (in the track's key; undo brings the old one back).
  • JUNO: the Juno-106 voice, six voices. The track's ENV is the Juno's envelope (ENV > FLT is the VCF ENV amount), its LFO the Juno's LFO (SHP moves the pulse width). The Juno-106 chorus is an insert type (effects), on any track.
  • CZ: ATK DEC SUS REL do not shape a CZ voice (its envelopes are the tone's).
  • NOISE: MODE ANLG, DUST, LFSR (clocked by the key) or META; COLR tilts white to brown; FREQ and RES a filter.
  • DRONE: a note plays four sines that modulate each other's phase, after Caravel's DRONE (bonszaimusic), itself inspired by the LYRA-8. COUPLE sets how much (the track's SHP moves it), TOPO who modulates whom: PAIRS (1 and 2, 3 and 4), a CHAIN (1 to 2 to 3 to 4) or a RING; a change of TOPO crossfades. SPREAD opens the four from unison to 1 : 5/4 : 3/2 : 2, CHAR bends each sine toward a soft cubic (more harmonics in the coupling), DRIFT wanders each pitch slowly (deeper and a little faster as it rises), TONE is a dark-to-open low-pass (the track's FLT moves it). The track's DTUNE (VOICE 2) detunes the four generators. BEAT plays a binaural beat: the left ear BEAT / 2 below the note and the right ear BEAT / 2 above it, 0.05 to 40 Hz (THETA 6 Hz, ALPHA 10 Hz, DELTA 2 Hz, GAMMA 40 Hz). It works on headphones only, and only the mid of the two ears goes through the track's inserts and sends. With BEAT on the network runs once an ear (twice the CPU). HOLD ON: a key latches its note and the same key again lets it go; HOLD OFF lets the latched notes go. The sequencer's notes toggle the same way, so a sequenced drone keeps HOLD OFF and holds its note with TIEs. One voice: a note transposes the whole network.
  • PLAITS: four of Mutable Instruments Plaits' synthesis models (Emilie Gillet, MIT), with Plaits' own knobs: HARM (HARMONICS), TIMB (TIMBRE; the track's SHP and ENV 1's TIMB card move it) and MORPH. MODEL WAVSH: a triangle-to-saw slope (MORPH) through five waveshapers (HARM) into a wavefolder (TIMB). ADDTV: 24 harmonics under a bump spectrum: TIMB moves the brightest harmonic, HARM adds bumps, MORPH goes from a flat, wide spectrum to a single peak. GRFMT: two formants synced to the note (TIMB the formant, HARM the second one's interval, -2 to +2 octaves, below the middle the plain carrier bleeds in; MORPH the window's shape). PRTCL: random pings of six resonant filters, each at a random pitch around the note (TIMB the density, HARM how far the pitches spread, MORPH the resonance); a note strikes all six. AUX fades to the model's second output, as Plaits' AUX jack: the Serge-like folder on a sine (WAVSH), an 8-drawbar organ (ADDTV), the Z oscillator (GRFMT), the raw clicks (PRTCL). TONE is a low-pass (the track's FLT moves it), LPG closes it as the envelope falls (Plaits' low-pass gate), VEL lets the velocity open TIMB. Four voices a track; PRTCL and ADDTV are the heaviest on the CPU.
  • STRINGS, VPS, TERRAIN, WSEQ, VECTOR, WAVE are Clipper's engines (3dSam's Clipper, GPL-3.0), each its own builder item. Each ends in a 12 dB low-pass (CUT, RES) that the track's FLT moves. STRINGS: the 16', 8' and 4' registers come from one phase, so they stay locked as on a Solina; ENS and RATE set the three-line ensemble's depth and speed, SHP turns the saws toward squares. VPS: a sine read through a bent phase. D moves the bend along the period (the track's SHP moves it), V sets its height: below 1 a CZ-like wave, above 1 a formant at V times the note; VENV lets the envelope sweep V. OSC2 adds a mirrored second oscillator (DTN detunes it), SUB a sine an octave down. TERRAIN: an orbit circles over a surface (TERR, 8 of them) once a period of the note. RAD (moved by the track's SHP), X and Y place it; ORB squeezes it to a line or turns it into a figure eight; DRIFT moves its centre slowly, each note its own way. At the surface's centre some orbits sound an octave up. WSEQ: each note plays a sequence (SEQ, 16) of up to 16 steps, each its own wave, pitch and level, at the song's tempo (RATE 1/32 to a bar). XFADE fades a step into the next, LOOP plays forward, back, bouncing or once, START picks the first step, PITCH scales the steps' intervals (64 as written, 0 every step at the key). VECTOR: four single-cycle waves (A B C D, 16 to choose from) at the corners of a square; X and Y place the point that mixes them (the track's SHP moves X), MOVE sends it round a circle, a figure eight, the square's edge, a sweep from A or a random walk, each slow or fast (+). WAVE: a wavetable (TABL, 8 of Clipper's) of 16 frames that POS sweeps through, crossfaded (the track's SHP moves POS); DTN adds a second oscillator detuned up, SUB a sine an octave down; DRV drives the filter, KTR sets how far the cutoff follows the key. The tables are built without aliasing in the pool, one at a time: every WAVE track plays the first WAVE track's table, and a track turned to a new table is silent for a few milliseconds while it is built.
  • SID: Mini-Tone's port of reSID 0.16 (Stephane Schmitz; reSID by Dag Lem): the Commodore 64's sound chip, three voices a track as the chip has. WAVE is TRI, SAW, PULSE, NOISE or a combined waveform (TRI+SAW, TRI+PUL, SAW+PUL, T+S+P), which plays what real chips measured, not the waves added. PW is the pulse width (the track's SHP moves it: PWM). MOD syncs or ring-modulates the voice against a second oscillator RATIO semitones away. MODE is the chip's filter (LP, BP, HP and their mixes, OFF bypasses it), CUT follows the chip's FC register (the 6581's curve is uneven, the 8580's linear), RES has the chip's 16 steps, MODEL picks the 6581 or the 8580. The waveforms alias as the chip's do. The amplitude is the track's ADSR, not the chip's envelope.
  • PROPHET: a Prophet-5 Rev 4 style voice: Melodee's oscillators, Poly-Mod and Curtis filter (Kerem Kilic), with our controls. A is oscillator A's wave (SAW, PULSE, BOTH; the .S ones hard-synced to B), B oscillator B's (saw, triangle, pulse and their mixes), B FRQ B's interval in semitones, MIX the mixer (-64 A alone, 0 both, 63 B alone). CUT and RES are the Curtis 4-pole, with half keyboard tracking. PMOD is the Poly-Mod from B: up from 0 it bends A's pitch with B (bells, growls, with sync the classic sweeps), down from 0 it moves the cutoff with B. PW narrows both pulses (the track's SHP moves it). The track's ENV is the amplitude and, through ENV 1's FLT card, the filter envelope. Five voices. Our own presets; it plays no Sequential programs.
  • DIGITAL TONE (builder item DIG_TONE): ALG's T1..T8 are routings in the manner of Elektron's Digitone (ours; not affiliated with Elektron): a carrier C, its modulator A and a pair B (B1 B2, the same ratio), two outputs X and Y that MIX crossfades (-64 X, 63 Y), FDBK on one operator. C, A and B are the ratios (C sets the pitch), A LEV A's depth (steady; ENV 1's card moves it), B LEV the pair's depth, which follows the track's ENV. The classic routings 1..8 sound as before; on them EDIT 8 is "-".
  • STRETCH: extreme time stretching of the SAMPLE material in the manner of Paul Nasca's PaulStretch (our code; AEther, an fm1-base app, was the reference). The source is read through a 4096-sample window, every frequency bin is turned by a random phase and the result overlapped every 46 ms: the transients smear into a smooth, endless sound. SRC is the sample set or user slot, POS where the scan starts, STRCH how many times slower (up to ~8000x; 127 freezes it), SMEAR the random phases (0 keeps them: a plain, phasey stretch), TONE a low-pass on the spectrum (the track's FLT moves it), SUB and SHIM an octave below and above, BLUR tones into noise bands. Every voice plays the zone of its part's newest note at its own pitch. One part plays STRETCH at a time (it holds 45 KB of the pool once); a second part on STRETCH waits until the first is silent. Latency 46..93 ms: a pad engine.
  • CS80: a Yamaha CS-80 style voice after CS-1's (twochannels), with our controls: two channels a voice, each a VCO through a high-pass and a low-pass in series. I and II are the channels' waves (SAW, PULSE, BOTH, SAW+SIN with the sine added after the filters, NOISE), II FT channel II's footage against I's 8' (16' to 2', the fifths between), MIX the lever between them (-64 I alone, 0 both, 63 II alone). HPF and LPF are the two filters (key tracking 1:1), RES both resonances (the CS-80's filters never self-oscillate), RING the ring modulator's rate (0 off). The track's FLT and ENV > FLT move both filters, as the CS-80's BRILLIANCE and VCF envelope do; SHP is the pulse width, DTUNE (VOICE 2) detunes channel II up to ~20 cents, velocity opens the filters a little. Eight voices. Our own presets; not affiliated with Yamaha.
  • SAMPLE: SET plays a built-in set (PIANO, BASS, VIBES, HORNS, STRGS, FLUTE, SCRCH, PERC; the builder chooses which) or your USR1 to USR3 slots (drums and samples). In SD card builds SET's last value is SD, one sample from the card. On SD the SET knob scrolls through the card's samples: a list opens on the folder of the part's sample and stops at its ends, and the sample you stop on is the part's (heard alone while stopped). SAVE on a folder goes into it, and HOME goes up. SAVE on a sample puts the knob back on the sets. GRAIN's and STRETCH's SRC work the same way.

Voices

VOICES, the voice count

VOICES sets how many voices a synth track uses, from 1 to the engine's own maximum. It shows the number, and a new track shows the engine's maximum. The engine's maximum is 8 unless the engine caps it: ACID has 1, PHYS 3, DRONE 4 and JUNO 6. VOICES is card 3 of the VOICE 2 page (ALLOC, DETUNE, VOICES, DROP). It counts in the POLY and UNISON modes. It is stored with the project and is not part of a sound: a preset does not change it, and it cannot be locked.

Voice modes, glide, priority

The VOICE page has four cards: the mode, GLD (glide time), GLMOD and PRIO.

  • POLY plays each note on its own voice. When the voices are all busy, the lowest held note is protected and the oldest released voice is taken first.
  • MONO plays one voice, and every new note retriggers it; GLD glides to it.
  • LEG (LEGATO) plays one voice and does not retrigger while a note is held: the new note glides there when GLD is set.
  • UNI (UNISON) stacks VOICES voices on one note and spreads them in pitch: DETUNE sets the spread, up to about plus or minus 40 cents.
  • PRIO (LAST, LOW, HIGH) picks which held note sounds in MONO, LEG and UNI.
  • GLD is the glide time; GLMOD (RATE or TIME) says whether GLD is the time an octave takes (RATE) or the time of any glide (TIME). A sequencer SLIDE with GLD at 0 glides in a fixed short time.
  • ALLOC (ROT or REUSE), on VOICE 2, picks the voice for a new note in POLY: ROT rotates through the free voices; REUSE takes the idle voice whose last pitch is nearest the new note. With GLD on, the nearest-pitch rule is used whatever ALLOC says.

Modulation: two LFOs, two envelopes

LFO 1 and LFO 2

LFO has the cards RATE, WAVE (SIN, TRI, SAW, SQR, S&H, SLEW: a random value each cycle as S&H, reached by a smooth glide instead of a step), PHS (start phase) and FADE (fade-in). LFO 2 (always on) has RATE, WAVE, TRIG (NOTE restarts it with a new phrase, FREE) and POL (BI or UNI). Turn RATE down past its slowest free rate (0.05 Hz) and it locks to the tempo: one cycle is 4BAR, 2BAR, 1BAR, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 8T, 1/16, 16T or 1/32. It follows the tempo and an external MIDI clock.

ENV and ENV 2

ENV (ENV 1) is the engine's own envelope, the track's ADSR: ATK, DEC, SUS, REL. It always shapes the amplitude. ENV 2 (ATK2, DEC2, SUS2, REL2) is a second ADSR of its own, restarted by each note; REL2 at 0 takes DEC2's time. ENV 2 runs on every engine, and only while one of its slots acts. It exists in builds that have the ANALOG engine.

ENV 1 MOD: four depth knobs made for each engine

ENV 1 MOD (shown as ENV mod) has no destination pickers: its four knobs are fixed for the engine, each a depth from -64 to 63 (0: nothing), named after what it moves. FLT, SHP and PIT move the voice's filter, shape and pitch (PIT: up to about 31.5 semitones at full); the others move one of the engine's EDIT values for each voice as ENV runs. An engine whose EDIT values already shape its own envelope (ACID, CZ, FM6, PHASE's DCW) shows those values here too: turning them is turning the EDIT value.

Destinations: two free slots on one shared list

LFO, LFO 2 and ENV 2 each have a second page with two free destinations: DEST A with AMT A, and DEST B with AMT B (the pages LFO DEST, LFO2 DEST, ENV2 DEST). All three read one shared list of destinations:

  • PIT, FLT, SHP, AMP: pitch, filter, shape (the engine's SHP value), amplitude (on ENV 2 or an LFO: on top of ENV 1's);
  • RES (the engine's resonance), PAN, LVL (the track's level), ATK DEC SUS REL, ATK2 DEC2 SUS2 REL2, and E1 to E8, the engine's own EDIT values, named by the engine;
  • CHO, DLY, REV (the track's sends) and INS1, INS2 (the amount of the insert in slot S1 or S2); FLT B (ANALOG 2's FLT B FREQ);
  • the rate and amount of the LFOs and envelopes themselves (L1 RT, L1 AMT, L2 RT, L2 AMT, E1 AMT, E2 AMT): this is modulation of modulation. Nothing feeds back into a runaway: every move starts from the authored value, from the modulators' values of the block before.

An LFO moves the value for the block; an envelope moves it for each voice as that voice renders. An envelope on a track value (PAN, LVL, the sends, the inserts, an LFO's rate or amount) follows the track's newest voice.

  • ENV 2's DEST A is FLT at 0 on a new sound, on INIT and on every factory preset that does not use ENV 2: raise AMT A and the filter follows ENV 2. Elsewhere it is the nearest: WHEEL TOP, FM6 PIT, CZ DCW (E2). On ACID, FLT is the 303's CUT and RES its RESO.
  • DROP is a fast pitch fall from each note's start. It is its own value (VOICE 2, card 4), not a destination.
  • The accent's filter kick (velocity above 110) uses ENV.
  • A drum lane has its own LFOs (LFO, LFO 2) and second envelope (ENV2), each with two destination slots, per hit.

Presets, favorites, the user bank

PRESETS browses the sounds of the track's engine: its factory sounds, INIT, your presets of that engine and its own collections (the FM6 voice packs); SHIFT (OCT- held) + PRESETS moves to the next engine's sounds. SHIFT2 (OCT+ held) + PRESETS filters the list, one a detent, round: ALL, FACTORY, USER, FAVORITES, INIT; the popup list names the filter in its header, lit for a moment when it changes. There are 32 user presets; PROJECT > USER lists them, with INIT to start from the engine's defaults. A user preset's LOAD over an edited sound is asked in red. SAVE + a page button (ENV, LFO, EDIT, FX, SCL, ARP) stores the current sound as a user preset (NAME first).

About 90 factory starting points (more with the optional engines), each a full patch on one engine, level-matched. The sampled sets are free recordings (CC0: Versilian Studios VSCO-2 CE and VCSL, Sonic Pi).

Favorites (builder item FAVORITES) narrow the list to the sounds you use:

  • SAVE + PRESETS marks the sound that plays (turn right) or clears it (turn left). The FAVORITES filter (SHIFT2 + PRESETS) browses only the favorites. On PROJECT > USER, SHIFT2 + the ITEM knob marks a user preset.
  • A favorite shows a star after its name. Up to 64 factory sounds and any of the 32 user presets can be marked. The FM6 and CZ banks and the drum track have none.
